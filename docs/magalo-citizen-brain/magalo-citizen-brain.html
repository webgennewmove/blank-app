Executive summary

The Citizen Brain is a small, layered system that runs on the server and keeps each civilian's thinking cheap and explainable. It aims past CrimeLogic V2's basic pedestrians by giving every civilian a reason to be on the street, a believable way of noticing things, and reactions that differ by personality, while staying inside a modest performance budget.

  1. Everyone is going somewhere. Each civilian spawns with a short trip: out of a door, one to three stops at places that advertise activities (a shop window, a bench, a bus stop), then out through another door. Purpose replaces wandering, and trips give every civilian a natural two-to-six-minute lifespan.
  2. They notice gradually. Vision builds awareness over time from distance, angle, how a person moves and what the civilian is doing. A texting commuter notices you at 6 studs, an alert pensioner at 27. Hearing is event-driven: thefts, shouts and sirens reach nearby civilians after a human reaction delay.
  3. Five modes, scored choices. A tiny state machine decides what kind of situation the civilian is in: Ambient, Alert, Panic, Victim or Recover. Inside each mode, utility scoring picks among four to eight actions using the civilian's personality. Modes keep behavior predictable and debuggable. Scoring makes individuals differ.
  4. Memory forgets on purpose. Every memory entry has a time limit. The only player-linked data is a live track that dies 7 to 9 seconds after the civilian loses sight of the suspect, plus an incident note that holds a place and a heading but no reference to the player. Both are deleted when the incident closes. Nothing survives despawn.
  5. The server thinks, clients perform. One central scheduler staggers brain updates at 0.5 to 4 Hz depending on mode and distance. Clients play animations, head turns, phone props and speech barks from a handful of replicated attributes.

No recognition, enforced by construction

The rule doesn't rely on anyone remembering it. Brains never receive a Player, UserId, name or appearance. They only see anonymous observations. Live tracks use one-off handles that can't be matched across time or across civilians. All incident data is deleted on a fixed schedule, and nothing persists. Details and tests are in 2.6.

Deliberately left out: GOAP or HTN planners, behavior-tree frameworks, Sims-style needs, crowd-simulation algorithms, language models, per-player reputation and any persistence. Section 8 explains why each would cost more than it shows on screen.

Recommended first build: milestones M1 to M4, which together form the minimum viable brain in section 9. It runs on today's small map.

2.2 The brain in one pass

Your example, traced through the modules. A civilian is walking to a shop and a disturbance happens ahead.

  1. Ambient. The Activity Runner is executing GoTo(newsagent), step 2 of the trip. Locomotion follows a pedestrian-graph route.
  2. Sense. The Stimulus Bus pushes a theft event to everyone in range. The vision tick confirms line of sight, so the Perception Gateway gives this civilian a live track on the suspect, labelled with the incident.
  3. React. The event outranks the running action, so the brain wakes after its personal reaction delay of 0.3–0.8 s. Nobody snaps round on the same frame.
  4. Appraise. Fear and Alertness rise using the threat formula. The mode machine moves Ambient → Alert, or straight to Panic if Fear crosses the civilian's threshold while the danger is close.
  5. Choose. Utility scores the Alert actions with this civilian's traits: Observe, Move away, Call 999, Ignore and so on.
  6. Interrupt. The GoTo stops at its next interrupt point. The trip remembers where it was.
  7. Act. Say Observe wins. Locomotion moves to a standoff point 12–22 studs away, Presentation sets Pose = Watching and the head follows the disturbance.
  8. Settle. The suspect leaves. The live track dies when the lose-track window expires, Alertness decays, and the mode returns to Ambient (or to Recover after a panic).
  9. Resume. Utility picks Resume trip, or Go home if the civilian is shaken. The civilian walks on to the newsagent, or heads for the nearest exit.

2.3 Identity, goals and perception

Identity and personality

Each civilian is generated at spawn from an archetype: a mean trait vector with ±0.15 spread, a walking speed range, a phone habit, a trip urgency, preferred activities and a bark voice. The MVP ships three archetypes: commuter, local shopper and pensioner. Students, workers on a break and tourists can come later. Traits run from 0 to 1: Confidence, Caution, Sociability, Curiosity, Awareness. They're turned into parameters once, at spawn:

Varied but not random. Three mechanisms keep outcomes from being completely predictable without making them silly. A score jitter of ×0.9–1.1 is drawn once per decision episode (on a mode change or a new stimulus), never per tick. When the top two scores are within 10%, the civilian picks between them weighted by score. Each civilian has its own seeded Random, so any odd behavior can be replayed exactly.

Civilians have no names or display names. The names in section 5 exist only in this document.

Agenda: needs and goals

  • Trips. Origin portal → 1–3 stops → exit portal, generated at spawn. Stops are chosen from POI adverts by archetype preference × distance falloff × free slot × variety (no activity type repeats within a trip).
  • Two light drives fill the gaps. Restlessness rises while idle and eventually moves the civilian on, or gets the phone out. Social urge, driven by Sociability, starts a chat when another willing civilian is at a chat spot.
  • Points of interest (POIs) are tagged parts with attributes: Activity, Slots, DurationMin/Max, Advertises (errand, leisure, rest, social), Node, StandPoint and LookAt attachments, Indoor and Shelter.
  • On today's map: shop window (browse 8–20 s), shop door (enter, hidden 15–45 s, then continue or despawn), bench (sit 20–60 s), bus stop (wait 15–40 s), cash machine (6–10 s), wall spot (lean or phone, 10–30 s) and chat spot (pairs, 6–15 s). Doorways double as shelters in a panic.
  • Fixtures come later: anchored roles such as a shopkeeper, run on an AnimationController rig with no Humanoid, as Roblox recommends for static NPCs.
  • Why this scales to the future city: a new street only adds tagged POIs and pedestrian-graph nodes. The brain code doesn't change.

Perception

The shared Perception Gateway turns world state into anonymous observations. Its candidates are player characters, police officers and, for social use only, other civilians. It never passes on a Player object, UserId, name or appearance. Each observation carries: kind (person, police or citizen), position, velocity, distance, whether it's in view, approaching me, sprinting, how long it has lingered near me, whether it's in cover, and, only for the civilian who witnessed the act, an incident label on a live track.

Vision runs periodically, on a budget. A squared-distance check runs first, then a field-of-view check (skipped inside a 6-stud peripheral bubble, where awareness is 360° at half strength), then a line-of-sight raycast from head to upper torso. Visible people build awareness:

awareness rate = attentiveness × 1 / (1 + (d / 15)²) × angle × behavior behavior : sprinting at me 2.2 · walking at me 1.3 · walking past 0.8 · standing 0.5 · crouched or in cover 0.3 noticed : awareness ≥ 0.35 → glance, head starts tracking focused : awareness ≥ 0.70 → appraised as a possible threat unseen : awareness halves every 2 s

For a player walking straight at them at 16 studs/s, the three example citizens notice at very different distances: Priya, texting, at 5.9 studs; Des at 25.1; Edith at 27.6 while walking and 21.5 while browsing a shop window. Approach from behind and nobody notices until you're inside their 6-stud bubble. That gives players a readable skill: come from behind, don't sprint, pick the phone-absorbed.

Hearing is event-driven. The Stimulus Bus delivers each event to civilians within its radius using a 32-stud grid, and each recipient wakes after its own reaction delay.

2.4 Affect, decisions and memory

Affect

Three numbers describe how a civilian feels: Alertness and Fear (0–1, fast) and Shaken (0–1, slow).

threat = severity × proximity(d) × visibility × approaching × police proximity = 1 / (1 + (d / 12)²) visibility: seen 1.0, heard 0.6 approaching: 1.5 if moving at me police: 0.6 if an officer is within 30 studs Fear ← Fear + (1 − Fear) × (1 − e^(−threat × FearGain)) decay : value(t) = base + (v − base) × 0.5^(Δt / halfLife), computed only when read Shaken = episode × (1 − 0.5 × Confidence); robbed 1.0, panicked nearby 0.8, saw it 0.5, heard it 0.2

Decay is lazy. Values are stored with a timestamp and worked out when read, so a calm civilian costs nothing. Shaken fades linearly over the shaken duration, raises baseline fear by up to 0.15, and tilts Recover toward going home. Habituation stops players farming panic: each repeat of a low-severity alarm within 30 s adds only 0.6× as much as the one before, and sprinting near a civilian raises Alertness but never Fear.

The enter and exit thresholds differ (hysteresis). Panic is deliberately hard to enter, needing both fear and present danger, and hard to leave, needing both calm and safety. That is what stops flickering.

U(action) = W(personality) × c₁ × c₂ × c₃ each consideration is a 0–1 response curve running action × 1.15 momentum episode jitter × 0.9 to 1.1, drawn on mode change or new stimulus switch only if : the running action finished, OR a higher-level event preempts it, OR after its minimum time a rival beats it by 20%
  • Interrupt levels: Ambient 1, Alert 2, Panic 3, Victim 4. An event preempts the running action only if its level is higher.
  • Cooldowns: Shout 10 s, Check phone 20–40 s, Chat 60 s. Call 999, Film, Chase, Statement and Tell someone happen once per incident per civilian. Keep distance can run at most three times in a row before the civilian moves on.
  • Role slots, issued per incident by the Incident Service: 1 chaser, 1 shouter, 2 callers, 3 filmers, 6 observers. Without them everyone does the top-scoring thing at once.
  • Two channels. The body channel is exclusive and scored: walk, sit, flee. The attention channel is an overlay chosen by salience thresholds: head-look target, glance, nod or bark, guarding belongings. Head turns never compete with walking, which avoids a classic utility-AI failure where a civilian stops dead just to look at something.

2.6 How the no-recognition rule is enforced

  1. Identity firewall. The Perception Gateway is the only code that touches characters. Brains receive anonymous observations: position, motion, pose, distance. Never a Player, UserId, name, display name, outfit or face.
  2. One-off track handles. A live track exists only for a civilian who saw the act or was robbed. Its handle is an opaque number unique to that civilian and that sighting. Lose the suspect for longer than the window and the handle dies. A later sighting of the same player creates nothing: there is no label to re-attach. Handles can't be compared across civilians or across time.
  3. Incident notes hold places, not people. A witness can still say "they ran off that way" after losing sight, because the note keeps a position, a heading and a time. It has no character reference and no appearance, so it can't match anyone.
  4. Hard deletion points. Live track: the earliest of the lose-track window, incident closed, character removed or despawn. Incident note: handed to police, incident closed, or 120 s. This is stricter than the rule requires: the rule allows tracking until the incident ends, and the design drops the live link sooner so that a long chase can't produce a "that's them again" moment.
  5. No persistence. The civilian system has no DataStore or MemoryStore access. Pooled models get fresh traits, a fresh outfit and empty memory on every spawn.
  6. The police keep their own case. Reports hand incident data to the Incident Service, which forwards it to the wanted system and police AI under their existing rules. The civilian's copy is deleted on handover. Police pursuit is never affected, because it doesn't depend on civilians remembering anything.
  7. Lines can't imply recognition. Barks are present-tense and situational: "Oi!", "Someone call the police!", "They ran off that way!". An automated lint fails the build on patterns such as "again", "remember", "last time", "that's the one" or "I know you".
  8. Tested, not trusted. A unit test asserts that after IncidentClosed no brain holds an Instance reference or a handle for that incident. A recognition probe has the offender and a fresh player approach the same victim 20 times each with identical movement. Reaction distributions must match within noise.

Civilians can still remember their own condition. A victim stays shaken for a few minutes and has no phone. That makes them warier of everyone, equally, and most shaken civilians head home, so re-encounters are rare.

2.7 Navigation

  • PedNet, an authored pedestrian graph. 40–120 nodes cover today's map. Node kinds: pavement, corner, kerb, door or portal, POI anchor. Edge kinds and base costs: pavement 1.0, crossing 1.1, alley 1.3 (×4 for cautious civilians, ×8 when afraid), plaza 1.0, jaywalk 3.0 (only with no traffic and Caution under 0.4).
  • Authoring. A PedNet folder of small anchored invisible parts with CanCollide, CanQuery and CanTouch off, linked by ObjectValues, with a Width attribute. A Studio-only visualizer draws Beams between linked nodes while you edit.
  • Routing. A* in Luau. Edge cost = length × kind cost (from personality and mood) × dynamic cost: blocked edges ×10 for 20 s, edges near a disturbance ×5 for 60 s.
  • Lanes. Each civilian keeps a lateral offset within the pavement width, stable for the trip, with a slight keep-left bias. When two walkers meet head-on within 5 studs both shift, and the more cautious one shifts more.
  • Intersections. Corners connect to kerb nodes, and kerb pairs connect by crossing edges. Before stepping off, civilians do a quick "look right, look left" head turn. It's cheap and reads as very British. Signals are respected if the map has them.
  • Last metre. POI stand points are authored to be reachable in a straight line from their node.
  • Off-graph moves (observe standoff, chase, getting back onto the graph after being shoved) use PathfindingService through the Path Queue: at most 2 jobs in flight, at least 1 s apart per civilian, agent radius 1.5–2, height 5, no jumping or climbing. PathfindingModifier labels give Road a cost of 5 and Alley 2. Path.Blocked triggers a recompute only when the blocked waypoint is ahead.
  • Flee targets come from the graph: nodes 30–70 studs away in the direction away from the threat, reached by a route that avoids the threat's radius, with shop doorways preferred. Panic never calls PathfindingService, so twelve civilians panicking at once costs nothing extra.
  • Movement. Humanoid:MoveTo per graph segment, with segments short enough to finish well inside MoveTo's 8-second timeout. Arrival is detected by MoveToFinished plus a distance check. Roblox's current pathfinding guide drives agents with Humanoid:Move and a Heartbeat check instead. Either works; pick one and keep it inside Locomotion.
  • Stuck ladder. No progress for 1.5 s while moving → sidestep 2–3 studs → re-plan from the nearest node with that edge ×10 → choose a different stop → after 10 s, teleport to the nearest node only if no player within 60 studs has line of sight; otherwise despawn when unseen.

2.8 Action execution

Activities are short step lists. Step types: GoTo, Face, Loop(anim, duration), Gesture, Bark, Wait, UsePOI, SetProp, Enter, Despawn. Each step says whether it's an interrupt point, and a trip resumes from the last completed step. The brain never touches a Humanoid directly. Only Locomotion calls MoveTo, and only Presentation writes attributes.

Performance and scalability

Brain logic is the cheap part: a think is a few dozen multiplications. The real costs are Humanoid physics, replicating moving NPCs to every client, client-side animation on phones, and pathfinding bursts. The design spends its effort there.

On today's small map nearly everyone is Hot or Warm. Cold matters once the city grows.

6.3 Server authority, client presentation

  • Scheduler. One Heartbeat connection drives everything. A due queue holds each brain's next think time, starting from a random phase. Each frame thinks for up to 1 ms and carries the rest over, Victim and Panic first. Event wake-ups jump the queue after the reaction delay. No per-civilian loops and no while true do task.wait().
  • Physics. Civilians are server-owned (SetNetworkOwner(nil)). That stops exploiters simulating or flinging them and avoids the ownership hand-off stutter when players walk close.
  • Animation on clients. Roblox's performance guide recommends playing NPC animations on the client and creating the Animator there, because server-side NPC animation is simulated on the server and then replicated. The server template ships with no Animator and no Animate script. Each client's controller creates a local Animator when a civilian streams in, caches its tracks, and plays them from attributes. Verify early with a two-client test that animations appear on both clients and nothing replicates back.
  • Head turns, props, speech. IKControl head-look within 50 studs; the phone prop welded locally; barks via TextChatService:DisplayBubble plus a 3D sound. Idle variations stop beyond 80 studs.
  • Inputs. Clients send nothing about civilians except the existing theft request, which the server validates.
  • Late joiners see the right poses because state lives in attributes, not one-off events.

6.4 Replication budget

  • Attributes change only when state changes: Mode, Pose, Gesture, LookAtPart or LookAtPos, Prop, HasPhone, PhoneOut. Look targets update at most 2 Hz and clients smooth them.
  • Barks go through one RemoteEvent carrying bark ids, not text, limited to one per civilian per 4 s and six per second per server.
  • No per-frame remotes and no server-side tweens.
  • Physics replication of moving civilians grows with population × clients. Community reports describe network load becoming the bottleneck with many NPCs even when script time is low, so the streaming radius and LOD tiers matter more than Luau speed.

6.5 Humanoid setup

  • R15 (or R6 if mobile profiling demands it), no layered clothing, at most three accessories.
  • Unused states disabled: Climbing, Swimming, Flying, FallingDown, Ragdoll, GettingUp, PlatformStanding, and Seated unless benches use Seat parts. Health and name displays off.
  • A Citizens collision group that doesn't collide with itself: no clumping or gridlock. Civilians still collide with players and the world.
  • A fling guard resets any civilian whose velocity jumps past a threshold it didn't cause itself.
  • Vision rays use a RaycastParams collision group that ignores characters, so only walls and props block sight and no filter lists need rebuilding.

6.6 Efficient pathfinding

  • Routine walking uses PedNet A* in Luau over about a hundred nodes. It's fast enough to run synchronously, and results are cached by (from, to, cost profile) for 60 s.
  • PathfindingService is reserved for off-graph moves: at most 2 jobs in flight, 4 per second per server, 1 per second per civilian, and never during a panic. ComputeAsync yields, so jobs run in their own threads, and the start point is snapped to the nearest graph point first so the start is never inside geometry.
  • Roblox documents limits of 3,000 studs straight-line distance and possible failure beyond 20,000 nodes. These are irrelevant on today's map, but PedNet keeps long trips off PathfindingService entirely as the city grows.

6.7 Population, despawning and respawning

  • Target count: 6 + 2 per active player, capped at 12 on today's map.
  • Spawning: at portals at least 35 studs from every player and out of their line of sight, at most one spawn per 0.5 s. A door-open cosmetic on clients makes emerging look natural.
  • Despawning: when a trip ends and nobody is watching, or after 30 s in Cold.
  • Robbed civilians leave through Go home. Phones come back into the world through new spawns, never by restocking the same civilian.
  • The pool is built at server start, two models per frame, with 10–12 outfits. There's no ApplyDescription at runtime, because frequently respawning Humanoid models is expensive.

6.8 Bounded memory and leak prevention

  • Each civilian owns one cleanup bag (Trove or Maid) holding every connection, timer, POI reservation and queued path request.
  • Despawn order: cancel activity → release POI slot → cancel path jobs → clear memory and tracks → destroy the bag → park the model → reset attributes → return to pool.
  • Instance-keyed caches use weak keys. Global caps: 8 incidents, 256 cached paths, a 64-entry stimulus ring.
  • Clients clean up everything when a civilian streams out.
  • Soak test: 500 spawn and despawn cycles; instance count, connection count and Lua heap must return to baseline.

6.9 Scaling path

  1. 6–12 civilians: everyone Hot or Warm, full brains.
  2. 12–40: tiers, think, raycast and path budgets with carry-over, client animation LOD.
  3. 40–100+ across a bigger city: Cold civilians as data-only records walking their routes, materialized from the pool when a player approaches. Consider custom client-rendered crowds (server sends compact positions, clients drive rigs) only if profiling shows Humanoid physics or replication is the limit.

Risks and tradeoffs

A critical pass over my own proposal.

Overengineering risks, and what I cut

  • Needs simulation. Hunger and energy are invisible within a 2–6 minute life. Cut.
  • Planners. Civilian plans are 2–4 known steps. Cut.
  • Fine-grained personality. Nobody can tell Curiosity 0.42 from 0.47. Three archetypes with modest spread; the traits are tuning knobs, not content.
  • Gossip and rich memory. Civilians are gone in minutes. One "Did you see that?" is enough.
  • Realistic sound propagation. A radius is enough, with optional halving when occluded.
  • Crowd steering (RVO). Sidestep plus no-collide works at this scale.
  • Parallel Luau. Brains cost microseconds; the cost is in Humanoids and replication. Wait for profiling.

Impressive on paper, pointless in play

  • Long emotional arcs, daily schedules on a small map, civilians remembering events for an hour, lip-sync, NPC-to-NPC dialogue trees.
  • Witnesses so sharp that theft stops being fun. Role caps, notice delays and the texting-commuter archetype keep the core loop playable. Tune toward a healthy share of approaches going unnoticed until close, and watch it in playtests.
  • A guarded phone as a hard counter. Putting the phone away is cosmetic until you decide otherwise, because it would change theft balance.

Pathfinding problems

  • Thin pavements can fail the navmesh agent radius. PedNet keeps routine walking off the navmesh.
  • Kerbs and small props catch Humanoids. Keep edges clear, test kerb heights, and route tall kerbs through crossings.
  • Doors and interiors become portals that hide or despawn civilians until M7.
  • Players blocking doorways or POIs: slots time out, civilians try another portal, or wait 1 s and reroute.
  • Starting a path inside geometry fails, so starts snap to the graph first.
  • Pathfinding bursts during mass panic: panic uses PedNet only.
  • MoveTo's 8-second timeout: short segments, re-issued as needed.

Multiplayer edge cases

  • Two players rob one civilian at once: TheftService locks the target; the second finds no phone.
  • Thief resets or leaves: the character is removed, tracks drop, and the civilian-facing incident closes early. The police case follows its own rules.
  • Several incidents together: at most 2 live tracks per civilian; appraisal takes the highest threat; role slots are per incident.
  • Herding and blocking: Keep distance caps at 3 repeats, then Move on; the stuck ladder handles the rest.
  • Sprinting in circles to cause panic: habituation, and sprinting alone never raises Fear.
  • Physics griefing: server ownership plus the fling guard.
  • Report spam: 2 caller slots per incident; the adapter de-duplicates.
  • Crowded servers: distance culling keeps perception cheap; barks are rate-limited per server.

Performance bottlenecks, most likely first

  1. Humanoid physics and replication of many moving civilians.
  2. Client animation on low-end phones.
  3. Raycast bursts during incidents.
  4. PathfindingService bursts.
  5. Spawning cost, solved by pool warm-up.
  6. Attribute churn from look targets, solved by throttling.

Defer

  • Interiors, day-night schedules and traffic interplay.
  • Fixtures, place-based tension and the Cold tier.
  • Filming that affects the wanted level.
  • Dialogue between civilians, ragdolls, parallel Luau.
  • Custom client-rendered crowds.

Keeping civilians out of loops

  • Flee loops: after reaching a flee node, a civilian flees again only if the threat is within 25 studs and approaching. Otherwise fear decays and Panic ends.
  • Dithering: minimum times, momentum and the 20% switch margin.
  • Trip loops: no repeated activity type, at most 3 stops, and a 6-minute trip limit that ends at an exit.
  • Stuck loops: the four-rung ladder ends in an unseen teleport or a despawn.
  • Contagion loops: each civilian relays an incident once, for at most 2 hops, at half intensity per hop.
  • Personal-space ping-pong: 3 repeats, then Move on.
  • Chase and report loops: once per incident per civilian, plus slots.
  • POI deadlocks: slots expire at their maximum duration plus 10 s and are released on interrupt and despawn.

Tradeoffs I'm choosing deliberately

  • Authored PedNet costs map-authoring time but buys cheap, reliable, art-directable flow. Pure navmesh would be less work and look worse.
  • Hand-tuned numbers need playtesting. The scenario harness and seeded runs make tuning quick.
  • Trip lifetimes mean you rarely see the same civilian twice. That's good for performance and for the no-recognition rule, at the cost of street "regulars".
  • Server-owned Humanoids are simpler and physical, but replication cost caps population. The Cold tier, and later client-rendered crowds, are the escape hatch.
  • Stricter forgetting means a witness can't re-identify a suspect who reappears 30 s later, even mid-incident. Police tracking is unaffected because it never depended on civilians.
